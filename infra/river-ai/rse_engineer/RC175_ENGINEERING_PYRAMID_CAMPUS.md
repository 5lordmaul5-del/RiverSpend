# RC-175 — RSEngineer: Engineering Pyramid, Campus & Workforce Roadmap

Status: blueprint/proposal only. No hiring, facilities, funding, certification, or operational capability is implied.

## Mission
Build RiverSpend's multidisciplinary engineering organization through staged, measurable projects, responsible use of automation, qualified human expertise, and a culture of dignity, safety, accessibility, and learning.

## The pyramid metaphor—without exploitation
The “pyramids” are durable systems, infrastructure, knowledge and products. Workers are collaborators, not slaves. No coercion, unsafe work, unpaid compulsory labor, or deceptive recruitment. AI/automation are tools under accountable human supervision.

## Organizational layers
1. **RSEngineer Council & Program Office** — portfolio coordination, priorities, resource planning, risk registers, milestone reporting.
2. **Architecture & Systems Engineering** — systems design, interfaces, requirements, integration and lifecycle planning.
3. **Research & Innovation Lab** — exploratory research, feasibility, prototypes, experiments and technical documentation.
4. **Engineering divisions** — specialty teams described in RC-174 and registry.
5. **Build & Test Operations** — prototype assembly, instrumentation, verification, QA, reliability and safe test procedures.
6. **Field Operations & Support** — deployment planning, maintenance, technical support, monitoring and incident response.
7. **Knowledge, Training & Recruitment** — competency maps, mentoring, learning plans, hiring, partnerships and knowledge preservation.
8. **Safety, Ethics & Compliance** — independent review, security, privacy, safety cases, environmental and legal compliance.

## Staged build plan
**Stage 0 — Organize:** mission, registry, governance, intake, scope, ownership and resource estimates.
**Stage 1 — Digital foundations:** software, RiverSSAI/RiverAPI integration architecture, infrastructure, test automation and documentation.
**Stage 2 — Virtual labs:** simulations, digital twins, design tools and remote collaboration; no claims of physical testing.
**Stage 3 — Low-risk prototyping:** small, budgeted projects in electronics, robotics, audio, video and game systems, with safety reviews and qualified supervision.
**Stage 4 — Partnerships:** universities, technical institutes, laboratories, manufacturers and qualified service providers.
**Stage 5 — Specialized facilities:** only after funding, qualified staff, safety cases, permits, insurance and independent validation.
**Stage 6 — Productization:** verified prototype, reproducible test results, support plan, lifecycle and compliance documentation.

## Project intake & gates
Every proposal records: purpose, users, requirements, owner, specialty, budget ceiling, timeline, dependency, risk level, applicable law/standard, evidence, test plan, review authority and stop criteria.
- Gate A: feasibility and ethical/legal screening
- Gate B: design review and resource approval
- Gate C: prototype authorization and safety plan
- Gate D: test evidence and independent review
- Gate E: release/operations authorization (explicit human decision)

## Controls
- No autonomous approval for safety-critical, financial, medical, aviation, aerospace, industrial, or other regulated systems.
- AI-generated plans/code/designs are drafts; qualified people validate.
- Separate author, reviewer and approver where practical.
- Maintain versioned documentation, audit trails, budgets and incident reports.
- Stop-work authority for any participant raising safety or ethical concerns.
- Protect intellectual property, privacy, access credentials and confidential data.

## First 90-day low-cost roadmap (planning template)
Weeks 1–2: catalog specialties, define leadership roles as proposed positions, prioritize projects and establish intake.
Weeks 3–4: create skill/competency matrix and project scoring rubric.
Weeks 5–8: choose 2–3 low-risk digital or simulation projects; define requirements and test plans.
Weeks 9–12: document prototype outcomes, lessons, costs and next-stage recommendations. Recruitment and facilities decisions remain subject to resources and approval.

## Success indicators
- Projects with complete requirements, owners, risk assessments and test plans
- Verified test evidence and reproducible builds
- Budget and schedule variance tracked
- Documentation and knowledge coverage
- Safety/quality issues resolved before release
- Training and competency progression
- Partnerships and funding opportunities assessed without overstating commitments
